Our brains are story-telling machines and they really like a protagonist with a concrete identity. A prominent example of this is how we see ourselves. The "default mode network" is a linked system of brain regions that is like a continuously running loop. Your brain tells itself who it is, which makes it who it is. All traits about you have no physiological reason to attach to you, Its what neuroplasticity is all about, you don't have to stay the person who is late to class, it is the illusion of "who I am" in your head that allows you to slip into certain tendencies so easily. Your brain cherry-picks data and emotions to stutch a manufactured story about you that it tells you to make you you. An interpretation of this has also been creatively explored in the movie "Inside out 2", which shows how the same memories and emotions can be stitched together totally differently to give someone a an alltogether separate identity (it is fictional but the allegory stands). The point is that this process of stitching a story has way too much power over you. The same experience can be dealt with in so many ways when different perspectives(filters) are brought in. The process of stitching holds so much power because it gets to cherry-pick data and dictate the processing/interpretation of the data. Raw information supplies no meaning, so we filter out some stuff to work with our filterate. A quote popularly attributed to Hegel, "We learn from history that we do not learn from history", is often interpreted as Hegel saying that humans are doomed to repeat the same mistakes. But what he meant was far from that, He meant to say that every historical era is strictly unique, and the past cannot be used as a guide or template for solving modern problems. In his "The Poverty of Histricism", Karl Popper argues how bankrupt the idea of historicism is. Historicism is described as some form of determinism in the progression of humanity based on theories stipulated from history. I interpret it as making up a story, a narrative, based on historical data points, giving non-human entities a personality of sorts in the process. And I argue in favor of Popper that you can't predict the future from the past. You can't pretend to have a sort of "objective" "pediction" in the chaotic stories of identities. It often happens that people charachterize nations, markets, industries and even field of research to operate in a predictable manner under a det of charachter traits used as objective truths. Political leaders repeat this logic in deciding their policies and communicating them. In the 1800s western world, the ideas of democracy (where person could have an equal vote) was seen as absurd, since people were ill-informed and uneducated, and the idea of education reaching a layperson seemed out of a fairy tale. A stable economy without slavery was impossible for most people who looked at the thousands of years of histories of great civilisations, until it was abolished. The idea of a united India in its diversity was seen as a fantasy by so many in the 1950s and yet we see somthing else today. And when all things work out the other way, one counters by saying that they did not account for other "important" factors or else they too could have predicted what happened. This, I would argue is cherry-picking data retrospectively to fit a narrative.
