A good insight into the lives of professors and academics, given by their blogs and pieces of writing and work, gives me a look into their set of defaults in their life. This is the USP of mentorship before the modern world, where a student would basically see the living and operating habits of a professional: habits that are built over decades of operating.
Once a default is found it contains the wisdom of such multi-purpose utility that it became nearly a mistake every time someone challenged them in a world that was not rapidly changing. Generation by generation, though, sophistication compounded until the cycle of mentorship died or decayed. This is how PhDs really work under a lineage of grades.
Defaults lead to consistency and order in the way things are done. Good defaults lead to workflows that are faster, with output that is easily interpretable and easy to plug into some other ecosystem where it is being used (without this utility of the output conflicting with the workflow). It is a shared minima.
This lineage of compounding sophistication is an institution. It leads to a structure in a collective and structure within the self, both structures existing in conjunction to minimize the operating friction for both the collective and the individual. Basic clearly defined guidelines allow for operating behaviors where you don't have to think about the operating behaviors. It simplifies and reduces the number of issues or decisions to be dealt with. All the emphasis and focus goes to a few externalized issues and decisions.
This streamlining has the same benefits as the adoption of the assembly line by Ford and/or the adoption of just-in-time production in 1970s Japan. Removing all the decisions in the internal process leads to two things though:
1. It removes diversity, variation, and subsequent exploration through unintentional or intentional human choices.
2. If the defaults are bad their faults can get invisible under the larger structure designed around those defaults.
In a dynamic world number two is inevitable: the incompatibility of older structure not only with novel technology but also with novel societies, institutions, industries, products, services, tools, and so on. In such a case number one serves the purpose of allowing the evolution of these defaults: a dynamically adapting structure for the self and for the collective.
That's where I find it important for any organization to have contained experiments: a larger structure with capital and resources, providing that capital and resources to smaller structures that are cushioned by the larger structure to experiment, defy, and challenge set and unset defaults, to allow for a greater sophistication to exist.
