See, before, art was also funded top-down by kings and rich families and bigger institutional structures for the purpose of messaging and narrating the reality to the masses.
Post-industrialization we had a huge decline in handicrafts as a profession. Then post-World Wars we had design movements strongly against design for beauty, having design only be appreciated for when it was invisible and served a practical purpose, further establishing the factory manufacturing system - the assembly line, just-in-time production, globalization, containers.
Right now art is funded bottom-up. It has been made accessible to pay creators in the smallest amounts by the masses. Artists (example: musicians) now are reachable to everyone through global platforms and their revenue comes from their streaming numbers and performances, free from agendas and institution-pleasing behavior to much bigger extents.
Right now research is funded top-down by governments, rich businesses, and big institutional structures for the purpose of military and commercial objectives like tech and medicine, and for the social sciences it is a lot of ideology: ideological objectives.
Post-AI you see where this is going. We need to structure platforms where:
- research is close to the people
- its consumption, discussion, and application are public
- its makers are directly represented
- subsequently these makers have the direct profit from all consumption, discussion, and application
Instead of controlled risk, where the control lies above the researchers and the risk lies under them, we have complete ownership in the independent value chain: better risk-taking with more risk taken. The artist goes on a streak of wins and losses and the spectacle is public.
For this we need to make consumption and production of research cheaper and accessible:
- The production gets there with cheaper energy, labs, and experiments.
- The consumption, with high-level education and training of the masses for them to understand and participate. This is not directly possible with AI, just like how factories didn't create the demand for the amount it was able to produce by itself to justify and sell the production.
In light of the new world order post-World Wars, where everyone believed in trying out new economic and political structures, we had transformations in the economic conditions of the most massive scale of the global populace to make them participate in the markets forming (with the strong incentives for the big players to increase and cultivate a consumer market).
What made the reality possible was actually policies by government. The socialist policies post-depression in the U.S. created a world where a factory worker's job was accessible with a lower skill than a craftsman and it paid well enough to indulge in the consumer products that the factories were making.
The metaphor I'm making here is of art with goods and research/intellectual work with services, taking research as a service because it builds upon something older to give space to something new on top of it, continuously revisionist and an always ongoing process.
AI creates the factory for all this production but it doesn't immediately need to justify its surplus of supply with demand yet. We quantify the ongoing process of a surface of research with projects. There is the "I need something good enough to do this" demand, A, and then the leisure demand, B.
Now governments might have a limit on A, and so will businesses. In the context of services and research, even tech companies have a rapid innovation cycle over a few years in their early days, after which they settle on the form factor of the product they want to sell (for example, phones and laptops).
For new innovations on new tech and old tech, we need new and more businesses, respectively. When everyone has a home we then reach a new saturation limit to the number of chairs we can sell. Similarly when we have everyone get access to services, we reach a limit on the practical applications of research, like how physics and construction don't need anything more than classical mechanics but there is still innovation on materials.
We then have unanswered questions about the world, tackled with B, out of pure human joy, to give us something consumed for pure human joy. Yes I kinda
claim there is a limit to the practical applications of research, so a limit to how tech will affect us, and this I anticipate since we have brilliant theories and bodies of work that work fantastically (where we don't have to go into the pcos and petas in scale, which is the scale of humans).
There will always be unanswered questions in research when you zoom out or zoom in more and more. Humans will want to gain that understanding and that's where we will have to be. We will have the artist, the researcher, asking the questions and being the director and navigating the journey of the answer: not to reach the objective destination, but to feel the flavors of insights that the artist seeks in the pockets along the way that oozes out his style, his unique seeking sight.
For this journey of saturating A to a comfortable level and moving on to B, we need to make consumption and production of intellectual work by individuals cheaper and accessible. The production gets there when energy is cheap and both consumption and production get there with education at a graduate++ level being cheap, which is only possible with policy and the biggest players in the world acting towards it.
Other points:
- AI won't become a participating consumer with B demand since the infrastructure that AI runs on and with is owned by some human who is paying for it.
- Can AI be an independent owner of physical goods and infrastructure in the world? Well if policy allows them the rights and freedoms of humans, we need to have the masses support it, which is when they empathize with AI. That is when they see AI as a conscious being with emotions, which is a fantastic direction of thought when we will have cyborgs.
